# approval-atlas
Cross-jurisdiction explorer of approved medicines: approval dates, sponsors, therapeutic areas and drug classes across EMA, FDA and other regulators, built from official public data.

**Status:** EMA (European Medicines Agency) human medicines only; Milestone 2 (enrichment, lifecycle events, MAH map) in progress. FDA and other regulators are planned.

**Site:** https://approval-atlas.vrognas.com

## Run locally
Requires Node.js 24+. R (with [renv](https://rstudio.github.io/renv/)) is needed only to regenerate the data: CI refreshes `site/public/data/` daily and commits changes.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev                         # open the printed localhost URL

# Optional: regenerate the data (don't commit it by hand; `git restore site/public/data`)
Rscript -e "renv::restore()"       # once
Rscript scripts/run-pipeline.R      # download EMA, MeSH (~313 MB unpacked, first run), ChEMBL and WHOCC ATC data, validate, write site/public/data/
```

Each run also reads up to 8 EMA product information PDFs (20 s apart) to complete incomplete or missing ATC codes; set `APPROVAL_ATLAS_SMPC_BUDGET` to change that number (`0` reads none). The results are kept in `.cache/downloads/ema-smpc/checks.json` and `site/public/data/ema_medicine_smpc_atc.json`, so a document is read again only when EMA updates it (or when an improved reader could find a code an older one missed: `smpc_reader_version` in `R/smpc-atc.R`). PDF text needs `pdftools` (on Linux: `libpoppler-cpp-dev`). Codes still incomplete after that can come from a hand-checked table, `curated_atc_codes()` in `R/curated-atc-codes.R`, each with its evidence.

**Modality** (what kind of medicine each active substance is: small molecule, peptide, antibody, siRNA, CAR-T, vaccine, radiopharmaceutical…, as 8 groups and 30 modalities) comes from, highest first: rows checked by hand (`curated_modalities()` in `R/curated-modalities.R`, each with a verbatim quote of at most 20 words from EMA product information, an EPAR, a ChEMBL record or PubMed); a radionuclide in the substance name or the ATC classes V09/V10; WHO INN stems (read as facts, see below), EMA's advanced therapy flag and "mRNA" in EMA's text; ATC classes WHO defines by product type (vaccines, immunoglobulins, allergens, coagulation factors) and EMA's text naming a vaccine or immunoglobulin; ChEMBL's molecule type; EMA's substance and pharmacotherapeutic group texts; ATC classes by use. What no source states stays "not classified". ChEMBL molecule types are matched on demand, not by the daily run: `Rscript scripts/match-chembl-modality.R` (after a pipeline run; about 1.1 s per request, responses cached in `.cache/downloads/chembl-molecules/<release>/`, so reruns need none) writes `data-raw/chembl-substance-matches.json`, which the pipeline reads. Definitions the taxonomy follows are cited, not used as data: a protein is "greater than 40 amino acids" in U.S. law (21 CFR 600.3(h)(6); the site's peptides are not limited to 40), and advanced therapy medicinal products and gene therapy (which excludes vaccines against infectious diseases) are as defined in EU law (Directive 2001/83/EC, Annex I Part IV; Regulation (EC) No 1394/2007).

Tests: `Rscript -e "testthat::test_local()"` and `npm test`.

- **Company groups** name the current owner of each holder (or, where a business was split, of the medicine's business) as of the date shown; they are not an ownership history. EMA's holder name stays with every medicine. Holders that are regulatory service firms (representatives) are shown as such until the sponsor behind a medicine has been checked by hand.
- **Regulatory-protection dates** are not legal advice: data exclusivity and market protection are estimates computed by this project from EU central authorization dates, counted from the first central approval of the same active substances (earlier national authorizations are not counted). Spellings of one active substance with and without a salt or hydrate are joined only where an EPAR page shows they are the same substance (`curated_substance_equivalents()` in `R/curated-substances.R`, published as `ema_substance_equivalents.json`); brands of one application approved days apart count from the first of them. Where that first approval is another company's medicine and the company's own first approval came later, the data exclusivity and market protection ranges cover both counts, and the estimate is "unclear" where they disagree. EMA's data flags only generics and biosimilars as copies. Copies it does not flag (hybrids, and a generic and a biosimilar whose flags are false), checked by hand against their EPAR pages (`curated_copy_medicines()` in `R/curated-copies.R`, each with a quote from the page; published as `ema_curated_copies.json`), follow their reference medicine, or show "reference not found" when it is authorized only nationally (e.g. Buvidal, Colchicine Agepha Pharma, Cuprior). A paediatric-use marketing authorization (PUMA, e.g. Alkindi, Kigabeq, Slenyto) has data and market protection of its own, even when its application refers to a reference medicine, so it counts from its own approval and is never a copy (`curated_puma_medicines()` in `R/curated-copies.R`, each with a quote from its EMA public assessment report; published as `ema_curated_pumas.json`); **other hybrids, informed-consent copies, and generics or biosimilars EMA does not flag still count as medicines of their own and may show protection they do not have**, or an "unclear" status. Orphan market exclusivity ends at the Union Register's end date or, without one, its link date + 10 years + extension. **Patents and supplementary protection certificates are not included**: no open EU-wide source exists.
- **Time from opinion to EU decision** (`meta.json`: `opinion_to_decision`): the median and 90th percentile of the days from the CHMP opinion to the European Commission's marketing authorization, computed by this project from EMA's dates for the opinions of the last five years. A typical time, not a forecast for a pending medicine.
